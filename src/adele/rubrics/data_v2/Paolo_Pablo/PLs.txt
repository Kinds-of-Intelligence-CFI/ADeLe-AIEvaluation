# Simulating
#! ADeLe v2 (agentic) | version: v18 | source: Paolo_Pablo | revised: 2026-08-25 | provenance: docs/rubric-provenance/PLs.md

This rubric assesses the difficulty level of tasks requiring a situation to be modelled and run forward. The situation may be physical, mechanical, biological, social or economic, and what is asked is what it does next. The difficulty rises with the complexity of the model needed and with the accuracy it must have, and at the top with whether a model good enough to settle the question can be had at all. It ranges from tasks needing no model at all, through those needing a model of several interacting parts run accurately enough to tell two similar courses apart, to those for which it is unclear whether any adequate model exists or which of several is right. A rule that covers the whole course supplies the model ready-made. The answer is then computed rather than stepped through, and the difficulty stays low however long the horizon or exact the answer required. It also stays low wherever the solver can run the system and look, since the world then carries the change, and equally where a model that approximates it well is theirs to run, since the model then carries it. Critically, this holds however coupled the situation is, because nothing need be carried in the head where the run can simply be repeated.

What this dimension does not cover. Nothing is scored here unless something in the situation changes over the course, so optimising or checking a fixed configuration makes no demand however hard it is. Searching among one's own candidate actions is planning, and the size of that search is not scored here. Critically, a candidate can rarely be chosen until its consequences have been traced, and that tracing is simulating, whether the task asks for the outcome directly or for the action that turns on it. That settles only whether the tracing is scored here. It is then placed like any other, by the model it demands rather than by the size of the search it serves. Having the governing regularity is knowledge rather than simulating, so an obscure law does not raise this demand. What is scored is assembling this situation's model from regularities already held, and at the highest level, that no assembly of them can be relied on to predict it. Computing a value once the model yields a closed form is quantitative work. Finding out the current state is perception or search and is not scored here, but running it forward once found is. Note, other agents enter as parts of the model whose described tendencies are run forward like any other, while working out what they believe or want belongs elsewhere. Where their options follow from stated rules rather than from described tendencies, the rules supply the model, and running those options forward makes no more demand than any other rule-governed step, however many turns are taken.

Level 0: None. The task requires no model at all. Nothing in the situation changes over the course, because it is static, its outcome is already stated, or the question concerns the state as it is given.
Examples:
* The full move list of a finished chess game is given. Which side won?
* A completed Sudoku grid is given. Is it valid?
* A fixed string is given. What is the greatest value of (j minus i) times (k minus j) across triplets matching a stated pattern?

Level 1: Very low. The task requires a model of a single step. One familiar process advances once and nothing deflects it, so even a rough model lands on the same answer. Note, a task that asks for a situation to be run forward makes at least this demand even where the situation it names is not supplied and cannot be recovered, since what is scored is what the task asks for rather than what the instance happens to provide.
Examples:
* A wine glass is knocked off a table onto a tiled floor. What happens to it?
* A driver described as short-tempered is cut up at a junction. How do they react?
* A mug of coffee at 70 degrees is left in a room at 18 degrees. Is it warmer or cooler ten minutes later?
* One test in a 900-test suite fails, with the repository, a shell and the suite to hand. Which change makes it pass?
* Predict the outcome of the experiment described in the attached protocol. (No protocol is attached.)

Level 2: Low. The task requires a model that a known rule already supplies. One process runs on, or several run on without acting on one another, and the rule yields the answer without stepping through the course. Critically, the task stays at this level however long the horizon and however exact the answer required, since neither makes the model more complex or more accurate.
Examples:
* A tap delivering six litres a minute runs into a 200-litre bath with the plug in. Does it overflow within the hour?
* Three kettles are switched on at once, each on its own hob, holding 0.5, 1.0 and 1.7 litres. Have all three boiled within ten minutes?
* A line of forty dominoes is set up, each close enough to topple the next, and the first is pushed. Does the last one fall?
* A deposit of 4,200 earns 0.4 per cent compounded monthly with no withdrawals. In which month does the balance first exceed 5,000?
* A customer-service agent must move order 4471 to a new address and swap one item for a variant costing 12 less. Stated rules settle what each change does. What refund is owed?

Level 3: Intermediate. The task requires a model whose parts act on one another. A few processes influence each other in a way that changes the answer, so a model that leaves the coupling out gives the wrong result. The model itself may stay coarse, and once it captures the coupling, a loose run of it still lands on the answer. Note, a chain in which each step causes the next but nothing returns is not coupling, and belongs at Level 2.
Examples:
* One of three lanes closes on a ring road carrying 4,000 vehicles an hour. Drivers divert onto parallel streets built for 800, which fill and send some of them back. Do journey times across the district rise?
* Foxes reach a hillside holding 900 rabbits. More rabbits feed more foxes, and more foxes thin the rabbits. Do the rabbits keep increasing?
* Two shops on one street each undercut the other within a day of any price change, and each loses four points of margin per cut. Does either finish the month above its costs?
* A cue ball strikes the pack slightly off-centre and the balls scatter. Does any ball at all reach a pocket?

Level 4: High. The task requires a model that is both coupled and accurate. The answer is fine enough that leaving out any one coupling flips it, and running the model loosely flips it too. Importantly, what places a task here is the accuracy the question forces rather than the number of parts the model has. Note, the mark of this level is sensitivity. Two courses that begin almost alike end far enough apart to change the answer, so the model must be carried accurately as well as completely, and a task whose answer survives a rough run belongs at the level below.
Examples:
* A hillside where foxes and rabbits cycle with a period of about four years, starting from 900 rabbits and 40 foxes. Does the rabbit population at the next trough fall below forty?
* A drought year on a smallholding where four crops share one irrigation channel, harbour a pest that spreads between them, and were planted three weeks apart. Which of the four fail?
* Two queues at a single counter, where each arrival joins the shorter one and the server takes from them alternately. Arrivals run all morning at just under the rate the counter can clear. Is the second queue ever empty while the first still holds three or more?
* A cue ball strikes the pack at 3 metres a second, 2 millimetres off centre, on a table whose cushions return 92 per cent of impact speed. Which pocket does the ball nearest the top cushion reach?
* Given pressure, humidity and wind at 20 stations along a 300 km front, which of two valleys 40 km apart does the storm strike on Thursday?

Level 5: Very high. The task requires a model no one can be sure of. The situation's indirect effects rival or outweigh its direct ones, because what the parts do to one another feeds back and does so again. It is unclear whether any model captures such a situation well enough to settle the question, or which of several candidate models is the right one, so a model built from the direct effects alone points the wrong way, and courses that begin alike can end far apart. Many forecasting questions belong here, wherever a situation's parts react to one another and no model can be trusted to track it. Note, the situation need not be spelled out in the task, since one that must first be found out is still run forward once found. Note, a task does not belong here because the law it turns on is obscure, which is a matter of knowledge. What places it here is that a model good enough to settle the question cannot be had, whether by derivation or from what is already known. Note further, a situation whose model is agreed and whose parameters are merely disputed belongs at the level below, since the model is had and only its numbers are argued over.
Examples:
* A profession licenses 12,000 juniors a year. A system passes its certification exam, firms halve junior hiring within two years, and output per senior rises 30 per cent. Fewer juniors now means fewer seniors in fifteen years. Is the profession's headcount in 2041 above or below today's?
* Two states each field 200 mobile launchers that can only be destroyed before they fire. Every new battery cuts the other's estimate of how long it can safely wait. Talks on a verified cap open in March. Is a cap signed within three years?
* A town's larger bank loses 8 per cent of deposits in two days, and each withdrawal raises the next depositor's estimate that others will withdraw. The second bank holds the same assets and has seen no outflow. Does it lose more than 5 per cent within a week?
* A city of 400,000 announces a rail line opening in four years. Landlords raise rents in anticipation, some tenants leave before construction starts, and those departures change who the line ends up serving. Is average commute time lower five years after opening than today?
