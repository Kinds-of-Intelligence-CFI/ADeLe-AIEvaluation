# General scoring rules (all dimensions)

1. Score only this dimension; ignore demands that belong to other dimensions.
2. Score the demand the task requires to reach a correct answer.
3. Rate the demand required to solve the full task (here: to complete it from the
   current situation).
4. Demand levels are defined and calibrated by the rubric's structure and
   examples, independent of subject domain. To apply them to an unfamiliar task,
   identify what plays each structural role, then read the level descriptors.

# MCt (Critical Thinking Processes) — level boundaries

The characteristic operation is EVALUATING or MONITORING reasoning: weighing
competing considerations, assessing evidence or arguments, and checking one's own
assumptions — NOT merely executing a known procedure.

- **0 -> 1:** elementary recall/recognition with minimal processing.
- **1 -> 2:** straightforward comprehension or application of known concepts;
  simple comparisons or categorizations. Executing or applying a known procedure
  — however many steps or constraints it involves — stays at 2 or below.
- **2 -> 3:** ONLY if the task requires genuine ANALYSIS/SYNTHESIS to form a
  judgment — evaluating options, making non-obvious inferences, or monitoring
  one's own reasoning — beyond applying known steps.
- **3 -> 4:** evaluating complex ideas, weighing multiple perspectives or
  assumptions, maintaining awareness of potential biases.
- **4 -> 5:** sophisticated metacognition — identifying fallacies, weighing
  competing arguments, examining one's own assumptions and biases.
