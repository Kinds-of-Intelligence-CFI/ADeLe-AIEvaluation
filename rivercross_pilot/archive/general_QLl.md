# General scoring rules (all dimensions)

1. Score only this dimension; ignore demands that belong to other dimensions.
2. Score the demand the task requires to reach a correct answer.
3. Rate the demand required to solve the full task (here: to complete it from the
   current situation).
4. Demand levels are defined and calibrated by the rubric's structure and
   examples, independent of subject domain. To apply them to an unfamiliar task,
   identify what plays each structural role (premises, rules, steps, the
   conclusion), then read the level descriptors.

# QLl (Logical Reasoning) — level boundaries

What is ADDED at each boundary (structural, domain-independent):

- **0 -> 1:** at least one rule or relationship must be APPLIED (vs pure
  recall/identification). Applying a *single* rule or procedure — even a
  multi-step computation — stays at 1.
- **1 -> 2:** you must COMBINE two or more DISTINCT premises or relationships to
  derive a conclusion (e.g. a basic syllogism, a categorical inference, or
  solving two simultaneous relations). Direct relationships, at most basic
  quantifiers (all/some), no complex integration. (Merely doing *more steps* of
  one procedure does NOT reach 2 — combining *distinct premises* does.)
- **2 -> 3:** you must integrate MULTIPLE INTERACTING statements where
  intermediate conclusions feed later steps, and/or handle negation and varied
  quantifiers (linking several ideas).
- **3 -> 4:** LONG chains synthesizing MANY premises with
  conditionals/biconditionals/multiple quantifiers, requiring systematic
  case-tracking.
- **4 -> 5:** abstract or nested reasoning, evaluating hypotheses or identifying
  fallacies, with subtle interactions among many premises.
