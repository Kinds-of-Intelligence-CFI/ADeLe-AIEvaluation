## What did not pass, and what it teaches

One control failed: the hurricane-count item came back **3** (3/5/2), against its batch 2 and my
predicted 2. Judge spread tells the story: five of thirteen items drew spreads of 3+ levels. The
splits are not noise but two readings fighting:

- **Opus applies the standing-rate reading**: board firings, storm seasons, junta delays have base
  rates; the rate answers as well as anything can. It alone put Altman at 2, storms at 2, Myanmar
  at 2.
- **Sonnet applies the constructed-model reading**: it put every contested item at 5, and also
  storms at 5 — it never once invoked the standing-rate clause on these thirteen.

Both readings are licensed by the current text. Whether a "standing rate" exists for a
post-crisis board or a hurricane season is a judgment the rubric delegates without criteria, which
is the residual ambiguity Pablo's example exposed. The candidate guard, NOT written (stopping rule:
it needs his ruling, since it moves whole families): *a rate is standing only where it is
catalogued and agreed in advance; a rate that must first be constructed from the situation's
workings is part of the model, and makes the model's demand.* Under that guard Altman goes to 3+
(no catalogued rate for post-crisis boards), storms stays 2 (seasonal climatology is catalogued),
and Putin stays 2 (autocrat-survival rates are catalogued) — which matches the medians the panel
actually produced.
