**A3 held at 5, and the reason is sharper than either of my two guesses.** Sonnet:

> confirming reagent/temperature/timing only verifies procedural compliance with the written route,
> not that the action did what it was for chemically

That is PLe's own definition of a check — *information that establishes, without further work,
whether an action has done what it was for* — applied exactly. And it explains why A1 and A3 differ
without either being wrong:

- In the toxicology study the action **is** administering a dose to schedule. The log establishes it
  was done right. That is a check, so the task sits mid-scale.
- In the synthesis the action is **making an intermediate**. Confirming the reagent establishes an
  input, not the outcome. That is an observation, not a check, so the task stays at the top.

So Pablo's E2 = 3 and the rubric's Level 5 synthesis example are both correct, and the discriminator
is whether procedural compliance *is* what the action was for or merely an input to it. **No text
change is needed. My proposed re-key would have been a mistake, and this is the tenth time a worry
or prediction of mine has lost to the text.**

## The failure mode this round names

**Over-specification collapses coupling into arithmetic.** Three items now (the fishery, the
mortgage arrears, and to a lesser degree the reservoir in r59) were designed as coupled and scored
as rule-supplied, because putting the deciding quantities in also handed over the rule that links
them. There is a line between a quantity that *decides* the answer and a quantity that *supplies the
model*, and the concreteness pass crossed it repeatedly.

**This puts the rubric's own examples at risk.** The Level 4 queueing example now reads "Arrivals
come at 0, 40, 55, 90 and 95 seconds and each is served in 30", which is fully determined
arithmetic and may well score 2 under its own rubric. The other numbered Level 3 and Level 4
examples need the same check before anyone relies on them.
