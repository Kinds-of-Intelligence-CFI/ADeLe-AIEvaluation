## Why rule 1 failed, and why the text is not the culprit

T6 was the decisive item: a coarse question whose second-order effects offset the first. It was
meant to show that the re-keyed Level 5 no longer keys on precision. It scored 4, and both
stronger judges said why in terms:

- Opus: "the sign turns on magnitudes rather than on any unsettled modellability that Level 5 requires."
- Sonnet: "a settled supply/demand-plus-tax-incidence model is available, so it does not rise to Level 5."

They are right and I was wrong. A scrappage scheme has an established framework; what is disputed
is the size of the parameters, not whether the situation can be modelled. Under the epistemic key
Pablo chose, disputed magnitudes inside a settled framework are Level 4. **This is an anchor error,
the sixth of this stream, and the fifth time the text has survived one.** The rule is recorded as
failed rather than reinterpreted after the fact.

T4 and T10 were also my design errors, not text errors. T4's sphere has drag feeding back on
velocity, which haiku read as coupling; T10 said "inflows are given for each week", which sonnet
correctly read as handing over the coupling pre-resolved. Both items were built badly. Coupling
probes have now been mis-designed by me four times, which is worth treating as a standing hazard
rather than a run of bad luck.

pointer to Level 5. A forecasting question answered by a supplied rule stays at 2. Forecasting is
